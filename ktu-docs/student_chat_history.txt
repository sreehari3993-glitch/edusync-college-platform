Dynamic Programming (DP) is a powerful algorithmic technique used to solve complex problems by breaking them down into smaller subproblems, solving each subproblem only once, and storing the solutions to subproblems to avoid redundant computation. **Key Characteristics of Dynamic Programming:**

1. **Divide and Conquer**: Break down the problem into smaller subproblems that can be solved independently.
2. **Overlapping Subproblems**: The subproblems may have some overlap, meaning that some subproblems may be identical or have similar solutions.
3. **Memoization**: Store the solutions to subproblems to avoid redundant computation.
4. **Optimal Substructure**: The problem can be solved by combining the solutions to the subproblems.

**When to Use Dynamic Programming:**

3. **Complexity**: When the problem has a high computational complexity, DP can help reduce the time complexity by avoiding redundant computation.
4. **Memoization**: When the problem requires storing the solutions to subproblems to avoid redundant computation.

**Understanding the Law of Demand**

### Law of Demand

The law of demand states that, **ceteris paribus** (all other things being equal), as the price of a good or service increases, the quantity demanded of that good or service decreases. Conversely, as the price decreases, the quantity demanded increases.

### Exceptions to the Law of Demand

There are two primary exceptions to the law of demand:

* **Income effect**: When the price of a good increases, some consumers may be able to afford it by increasing their income or reducing their spending on other goods. This can lead to an increase in demand.
* **Substitution effect**: When the price of a good increases, consumers may substitute it with a similar good or service that is cheaper. This can lead to a decrease in demand for the original good.

**Key Takeaways**

* The law of demand is a fundamental concept in microeconomics that helps us understand how changes in price affect the quantity demanded of a good or service.
* The law of demand is not absolute and can be influenced by factors such as income and substitution effects.
* Understanding the exceptions to the law of demand is crucial for making informed decisions in the market.
