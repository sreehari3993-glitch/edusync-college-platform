SECTION 1: KTU ACADEMIC REGULATIONS & DEGREE CLASSIFICATIONS
================================================================================
• Degree Requirements:
  - B.Tech Regular: 160–170 Credits (depending on scheme).
  - B.Tech Honours / Minor: 185 Credits (170 base + 15 additional credits).
  - B.Tech Honours & Minor: 200 Credits (170 base + 15 + 15).
• Passing Standards:
  - Minimum 40% in End Semester Examination (ESE).
  - Minimum 50% in aggregate (CIE + ESE combined).
• Degree Classifications:
  - First Class with Distinction: CGPA ≥ 8.0 with zero backlogs across all semesters.
  - First Class: CGPA ≥ 6.5.
  - Second Class: CGPA < 6.5.
• B.Tech Honours Eligibility (Regulation R 16.8):
  - Must register along with 4th semester registration.
  - Must maintain CGPA ≥ 8.0 at the end of the 8th semester.
  - Must complete 15 additional credits across S4, S5, S6, and S7 without failing any registered course.

SECTION 2: CIE, MARKS & OFFICIAL CGPA FORMULAS
================================================================================
• Continuous Internal Evaluation (CIE - 50 Marks):
  - Series Exam 1: 15 Marks (Tests Modules 1 & 2)
  - Series Exam 2: 15 Marks (Tests Modules 3 & 4)
  - Micro-Assignments & Quizzes: 10 Marks
  - Class Attendance: 10 Marks
• CGPA to Percentage Conversion Formulas:
  - B.Tech 2024 Scheme: Percentage Marks = 10 × CGPA
  - B.Tech 2019 Scheme, MCA 2020, MBA 2020: Percentage Marks = (10 × CGPA) - 2.5
  - B.Tech 2015 Scheme, B.Arch 2016: Percentage Marks = (10 × CGPA) - 3.75
• SGPA Formula:
  SGPA = Σ(Course Credit × Grade Point Earned) / Σ(Total Course Credits Registered)
• Grade Point Scale:
  - S Grade: 10.0 (≥90%)
  - A+ Grade: 9.0 (85–89%)
  - A Grade: 8.5 (80–84%)
  - B+ Grade: 8.0 (75–79%)
  - B Grade: 7.5 (70–74%)
  - C+ Grade: 7.0 (65–69%)
  - C Grade: 6.5 (60–64%)
  - D Grade: 6.0 (55–59%)
  - P Grade: 5.5 (50–54%)
  - F Grade: 0.0 (<50% aggregate or <40% in ESE)

SECTION 3: ATTENDANCE, DUTY LEAVES (OD) & MEDICAL CONDONATION
================================================================================
• Attendance Thresholds:
  - Regular Requirement: Minimum 75% attendance in each registered course to write ESE.
  - Medical Condonation Zone (60% to 74% - Regulation R 6.2):
    Principal can condone shortage down to 60% on genuine medical grounds (hospitalization/accident)
    with medical fitness certificate. Maximum allowed in 2 semesters across the degree.
  - Critical Shortage (< 60%): Awarded 'FE' grade (Failed due to Eligibility). Must repeat course.
  - Semester Promotion Floor (R 7.32): Minimum 40% average attendance required to advance.
• Special Attendance Relaxations:
  - Menstrual Leave: 2% relaxation (minimum requirement drops to 73%).
  - PWD Students: 5% relaxation (minimum requirement drops to 70%).
• Duty Leave (On-Duty / OD):
  - Granted for representing college/university in hackathons, sports, NSS, NCC, or cultural fests.
  - Maximum 10% attendance relaxation for state/national representation; 5% for college events.
  - Multi-tier digital approval chain: Student Application → Faculty Advisor → HOD → Principal.

SECTION 4: ENGINEERING BRANCH COMPARISON & CAREER ADVISORY
================================================================================
• Computer Science & Engineering (CSE) vs. Civil Engineering (CE):
  - Computer Science: Focus on algorithms, software development, cloud computing, AI/ML, and cybersecurity. High placement drive volume, starting packages ₹4.5–₹25+ LPA, high remote work flexibility.
  - Civil Engineering: Focus on physical infrastructure, structural design, geotechnical systems, smart cities, and construction management. High job stability in core public sectors, CPWD, NHAI, Railways, and IES (UPSC) exams.
• Electronics & Communication (ECE) vs. CSE:
  - ECE provides dual career paths: Core VLSI/Embedded Systems (Intel, Qualcomm, Texas Instruments) + Software engineering.
• Mechanical Engineering (ME):
  - Core manufacturing, CAD/CAM, robotics, aerospace, electric vehicles (EV), and PSU jobs (ISRO, BARC, DRDO).

SECTION 5: CORE COMPUTER SCIENCE FUNDAMENTALS
================================================================================
• Database Management Systems (ACID Properties):
  - Atomicity: Transactions are all-or-nothing units.
  - Consistency: Transitions preserve all integrity constraints.
  - Isolation: Concurrent transactions do not read uncommitted dirty data.
  - Durability: Committed updates permanently persist across crashes and power failures.
• Operating Systems:
  - 4 Coffman Deadlock Conditions: Mutual Exclusion, Hold & Wait, No Preemption, Circular Wait.
  - Avoidance: Banker's Algorithm using Available, Allocation, and Max Need matrices.
  - Paging: Non-contiguous memory allocation dividing memory into fixed frames and logical pages.
• Computer Networks (OSI 7 Layers):
  1. Physical (raw bits) -> 2. Data Link (Ethernet frames/MAC) -> 3. Network (IP packets) -> 
  4. Transport (TCP/UDP segments) -> 5. Session (dialogue) -> 6. Presentation (SSL/TLS) -> 7. Application (HTTP/DNS).

SECTION 6: DATA STRUCTURES & ALGORITHMS (DSA) QUICK REFERENCE
================================================================================
• Binary Search:
  - Array MUST be sorted. Repeatedly divides search space by half (`mid = low + (high - low)/2`).
  - Time: O(log N) | Space: O(1) iterative.
• Sorting Complexities:
  - Merge Sort: Time O(N log N) all cases | Space O(N) | Stable divide-and-conquer.
  - Quick Sort: Time O(N log N) avg, O(N²) worst | Space O(log N) | In-place partition.
  - Heap Sort: Time O(N log N) | Space O(1).
• Dynamic Programming (DP):
  - Used for optimization problems with overlapping subproblems and optimal substructure.
  - Approaches: Top-Down (Memoization) or Bottom-Up (Tabulation).
  - Common Problems: 0/1 Knapsack, Longest Common Subsequence (LCS), Coin Change, Matrix Chain.

SECTION 7: CAMPUS PLACEMENTS, ATS RESUME SCORING & HR INTERVIEWS
================================================================================
• Present-Past-Future Framework for "Tell Me About Yourself":
  - Present (30s): Name, college (TKMIT), branch (CSE), GPA, and technical focus (e.g. backend microservices).
  - Past (40s): 1–2 key project accomplishments, hackathons, or internships with quantifiable impact.
  - Future (20s): Why this target company role aligns with your long-term career ambition.
• STAR Method for Behavioral Interview Questions:
  - S (Situation): Set the scene and background context.
  - T (Task): Specify your unique challenge or responsibility.
  - A (Action): Detail the technical and leadership actions YOU took.
  - R (Result): Conclude with quantifiable outcomes (e.g. "Improved throughput by 35%").
• ATS Resume Optimization:
  - Standard single-column clean format (Inter, Arial, Calibri fonts).
  - Include quantifiable metrics in bullet points (e.g. "Developed REST API serving 500 req/sec").
  - Include verified GitHub repository links, live project demos, and LeetCode/HackerRank profiles.

CASE STUDY 1: BRANCH & COURSE COMPARISON QUERIES
• User Query: "IS COMPUTER SCIENCE BEST OR CIVIL" / "WHICH IS THE BEST COURSE"
• Incorrect Initial Agent Behavior:
  - The agent attempted strict literal RAG grounding on KTU administrative syllabus tables.
  - Flawed Output: "Computer Science is not a Civil Engineering program. It is a program that falls under Mechanical department... Therefore, it is not possible to compare Computer Science with Civil Engineering."
• Root Cause: Over-constraining system prompts with "Answer strictly on verified excerpts only", forcing the LLM to hallucinate literal PDF matches for subjective/advisory questions.
• How It Was Corrected:
  - Upgraded RAG generation prompt with Gemini/Claude reasoning instructions.
  - Implemented multi-dimensional comparison tables: Focus, Industry Demand, Starting Packages (₹4.5–25 LPA vs ₹3.5–10 LPA), Government/IES vs Private Startup roles, and Personalized Recommendation criteria ("Choose CS if... Choose Civil if...").

CASE STUDY 6: ENGINEERING MATHEMATICS & LOG EXCLUSION FROM RAG
• User Query: "partial differenciation", "What is partial differentiation in engineering mathematics?"
• Incorrect Initial Agent Behavior:
  - When a student typed mathematical notes on Partial Differentiation, substring matching on "me" in "time" or "%" misclassified it as a personal attendance inquiry.
  - Furthermore, `RagService` was indexing `student_chat_history.txt` as a knowledge source and citing `student_chat_history.txt (Page 1)` along with generic microeconomics examples (Law of Demand).
• Root Cause:
  - `RagService.java` accepted all `.txt` files without excluding conversation transcripts and audit logs.
  - Substring checks in `isPersonalStudentQuery` lacked word boundaries.
• How It Was Corrected:
  - Updated `isSupportedFilename` in `RagService.java` to strictly exclude all chat logs, audit files (`student_chat_history.txt`, `student_chat_audit_log.jsonl`, `*.log`, `*.jsonl`).
  - Enforced strict word boundaries (`\b(my|me|i|am i|can i)\b`) for personal query detection so mathematical definitions are never misrouted to personal attendance records.
  - Raised RAG similarity threshold to 0.45 and mapped partial differentiation directly to B.Tech First Year Engineering Mathematics (Calculus, Multivariable Optimization, Wave Equations, Gradient Descent in AI/ML).

CASE STUDY 8: ATTENDANCE CONDONATION VS ACADEMIC BACKLOGS
• User Query: "What is condonation in KTU and how many times can a student apply?"
• Incorrect Initial Agent Behavior:
  - Hallucinated: "Condonation is a process that allows students to regularize backlogs and continue studies... one-time opportunity to condone backlogs".
• Correct KTU Regulation:
  - Condonation in KTU is strictly for ATTENDANCE SHORTAGE (Regulation R 6.2).
  - Eligibility: Attendance between 60% and 74% in a course due to genuine medical illness or authorized institutional duty.
  - Limit: A student can apply for attendance condonation for a MAXIMUM OF 2 SEMESTERS across their entire 4-year B.Tech degree.
  - Minimum Floor: Attendance below 60% cannot be condoned under any circumstances and results in an 'FE' (Failed due to Eligibility) grade.
  - Note: Academic course backlogs (F grades) are cleared through regular supplementary university examinations, NEVER through condonation.
